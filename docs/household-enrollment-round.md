# Household, Payroll, Education and Enrollment — September 9, 2026

## Delivered Scope

- Employer health-option JSON export/import uses a positive allowlist and an explicit review step. The export contains only numeric plan terms and fixed enumerations: premium, pay frequency, deductible/OOP and Rx sublimits, coinsurance, network and employer HSA funding/timing. It contains no original option IDs or labels, people, care/diagnosis/Rx entries, private coverage rules, personal HSA assets/contributions/allowance, tax assumptions, term quotes or other plan sections. Recipients get numbered options and must confirm their own coverage tier, drug rules and HSA eligibility. Unknown imported fields are ignored; wrong format/version, invalid values, oversize files and full personal plans are rejected. Nothing is uploaded.
- The gold star denotes the lowest complete **net economic cost** in the selected utilization scenario, with cent-level ties. It does not score network quality or recommend enrollment. All options must be complete before ranking. Cost bars explicitly show **Premiums + Care**, above their component caption; benefits and tax savings remain separate.
- A blank HSA tax-rate override can use a narrow 2026 federal wage-income estimate when reconciled household payroll supports it. Taxable wages already reflect traditional payroll savings; Roth savings do not reduce them. The calculation uses the standard deduction and computes the incremental tax difference across the proposed HSA deduction, so a deduction crossing brackets does not receive the top rate on every dollar. Manual values, including zero, always win. The estimate stays blank for incomplete/net-only pay, unsupported filing statuses, age-65+ cases, retirement during the year, a non-January/non-2026 plan year, missing opening year, benefits, conversions, rentals, cash-interest income or dated life events. Married households need explicit payroll entries for both adults. This is not a complete tax-return estimate; state/payroll taxes and special deductions are not inferred. Payroll must precede the HSA contribution being compared or the user must override the rate to avoid counting a deduction twice. The derived rate is recomputed locally for screen and PDF, not written into the manual override.
- Household retains self/partner timeline compatibility and adds anonymous dependent records. Each dependent has an optional college start/duration and qualified-cost goal. An explicit age-18 shortcut uses the entered birth year. Covered people can optionally link to household members; this alone establishes neither tax dependency nor health/HSA eligibility.
- Cash & Investments adds dedicated 529 records linked to dependents. Multiple accounts for one beneficiary share **one** goal. Plan Summary and its PDF show college-start assets, total costs, additional funding needed, native SVG bars and an annual ledger. 529 balances are deliberately outside retirement withdrawal funds and retirement net-worth totals.

## Contribution Builder

The 401(k)/403(b) builder previews a fixed annual amount, gross-pay percentage or maximum for the selected supported tax year. Every quantitative input is blank initially. The selected year is currently 2026 only; the app never invents future legal limits.

2026 employee deferral limit: $24,500. Optional year-end age-50+ catch-up: $8,000, replaced by $11,250 at ages 60–63. Shared-limit deferrals elsewhere reduce remaining capacity. Prior-year Social Security wages **from this employer** above $150,000 force this job's modeled catch-up into Roth. Exactly $150,000 does not. The builder conservatively allocates this job after entered other deferrals; exact cross-employer catch-up attribution needs review. Compensation and the $72,000 regular annual-additions cap are checked; eligibility, plan-specific lower limits and permitted catch-ups remain user confirmations.

Employee Roth percentage leaves the remainder traditional. Employer funding is a confirmed annual dollar amount, not a guessed match formula; its Roth percentage is independent of employee elections. Roth employer funding requires confirmation that the plan permits fully vested allocations. It is added to ordinary taxable income separately from taxable wages, with no assumed withholding. Traditional employer funding is not an employee deduction.

Applying the preview replaces this pay entry's savings, taxable wages, take-home pay and account allocations. Traditional and Roth destinations must have matching owner and tax treatment. Payroll gross minus withholding, other deductions and employee savings must equal take-home. Other pretax deductions are a subset of Other Payroll Deductions. Employer funding does not reduce the employee's deposit. Monthly transfers and taxable income reconcile to the shared ledger. Manual edits causing allocation mismatches block projection until corrected. Split payroll selects the monthly Summary/PDF engine.

Actual anchored paycheck counts are used for the selected year, including a 27-pay biweekly year. Without dates, the existing frequency convention applies. Applied per-paycheck amounts remain fixed nominal assumptions in later years; they are not an automatic annual-limit enforcement service. The retirement calendar stops pay. Reapply after pay/frequency/deduction changes and review new tax years. SIMPLE plans, special service catch-ups, non-Roth after-tax contributions, complex annual-additions aggregation, plan compensation definitions and withholding calculation remain excluded. Employer Roth tax reporting follows the newer SECURE 2.0 guidance; some older IRS FAQ text still describes the prior rule.

## Education Calculation Contract

Opening 529 balances use the Household opening-balance year. Contributions are deposited at the start of each month before the college start year. Each account's monthly compound return is `(1 + annual return)^(1/12) - 1`; no contributions are presumed during college. Cost goals are entered in opening-year dollars and compounded once to each college year. Annual qualified costs are drawn at the beginning of each college year, proportionally from available linked balances. The ledger conserves opening balance + deposits + return − withdrawals = closing balance. A missing goal/input or beneficiary link produces review status, not a zero-cost success result.

**Add / Update Budget Provision** is an explicit action. It creates or updates one canonical monthly budget row per 529, continuing through retirement when applicable and ending before college. It does not remove unrelated manually entered saving. Education projection deposits assume funding; this standalone projection is not dynamically reduced by a household funding shortfall. A budget provision is a cash outflow to an education asset outside the retirement portfolio, not a second retirement investment. Goal/account edits require explicitly updating the provision; removals retain existing rows for review. College withdrawal gaps are reported separately and are not silently charged to retirement cash flow. Education-only edits do not stale household scenarios until a budget provision changes.

This scope models an entered **qualified postsecondary cost** goal. It does not decide expense eligibility, deduct contributions federally, infer state deductions/credits, or calculate nonqualified withdrawal taxes, gift-tax elections, scholarships, student aid, beneficiary changes or education-credit optimization. Users enter costs net of scholarships and expenses allocated to education credits.

## Authoritative Sources

Reviewed September 9, 2026:

- [IRS Notice 2025-67](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-67.pdf), effective January 1, 2026: elective deferral, age catch-up, annual-additions and prior-year wage thresholds.
- [IRS Catch-Up Contributions](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-catch-up-contributions): year-end ages, compensation limits and the 2026 Roth requirement.
- [IRS SECURE 2.0 W-2/1099-R guidance](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/secure-2-point-0-act-changes-affect-how-businesses-complete-forms-w-2) and [2026 Form 1099-R instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099r): Roth employer allocations as taxable income, separate from W-2 elective deferrals; references Notice 2024-2, questions L-1 through L-11.
- [IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf): 2026 standard deductions and federal brackets. This reuses the existing dated federal module.
- [IRS Topic 313](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc313) and [Publication 970 (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p970): contributions are not federally deductible; tax-free qualified distributions require eligible costs and coordination with education credits.
